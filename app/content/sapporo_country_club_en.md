Sapporo Country Club sits inland from central Sapporo in a forested pocket of Hokkaido, and it's one of the region's older clubs. That history shows up less in ceremony and more in mature trees, settled fairway lines, and a clubhouse that feels lived-in rather than showy.

- Players who enjoy tree-lined, strategic golf where positioning off the tee matters more than raw distance

## Course Overview

The layout runs through stands of Hokkaido forest, so most holes are visually separated from one another, which keeps the round feeling private even when the club is busy. Fairways are generally forgiving in width but the tree lines punish anything that strays too far, so shot placement matters more than pure power. Elevation change is moderate rather than dramatic, with enough movement to keep the round interesting without wearing out your legs.

Turf conditions follow the Hokkaido calendar: fairways and greens are typically at their best from midsummer into early autumn, after the spring recovery period. Wind is usually lighter here than at coastal Hokkaido courses, since the tree cover breaks up gusts, but open stretches can still catch a crosswind. Greens tend to be medium-paced and read fairly true, rewarding steady lag putting over aggressive charges. Expect a course that plays fair to sound course management and is less forgiving of wayward tee shots than its calm scenery might suggest.

## Access

The nearest major gateway is New Chitose Airport, with a drive to the Sapporo area that typically runs about an hour depending on traffic. From central Sapporo itself, the club is a shorter drive, generally well under an hour, making it a realistic half-day outing if you're based in the city. Public transit does not reach the clubhouse directly, so a rental car or taxi is the practical way in. A taxi works fine for a one-off round, but if you're playing multiple courses around Hokkaido during your trip, renting a car will save money and give you more flexibility with tee times.

## Dress Code & Tips

Standard Japanese golf club etiquette applies: collared shirts, tailored trousers or golf shorts, and no denim on the course. Soft spikes are required, and most golfers rent shoes on-site if they didn't pack their own. A few practical tips for the visit:

- Arrive with enough buffer to check in, change, and warm up, since Japanese clubs often run tee times on a tighter schedule than you might expect
- Keep pace with the group ahead of you; slow play is noticed here more than at casual public courses
- Bring layers even in summer, since Hokkaido mornings can be cool before the day warms up, and pack for possible rain given the forest setting
