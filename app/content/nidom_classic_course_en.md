## Course Overview

Nidom Classic Course sits inland from Tomakomai in a forested stretch of Hokkaido, and the wooded setting shapes most of what you'll notice on the ground. Fairways are cut through stands of trees, so shot shape and club selection matter more than raw length — tee shots reward accuracy over brute distance, and mis-hits into the tree lines can cost a full stroke or more in recovery. Elevation change is moderate rather than dramatic, giving a sense of movement hole to hole without turning the round into a hiking trip.

Turf conditions in Hokkaido follow a shorter growing season than courses further south, so expect firmer, cooler-season grass character for much of the year, with the best playing surfaces typically in mid-to-late summer. Wind is usually manageable given the tree cover, though open stretches can still catch a crosswind — worth asking your caddy or starter about at check-in. As a resort-style layout, the course is generally maintained for a broad range of handicaps rather than set up as a pure championship test, which makes it a comfortable round for mixed-ability groups.

## Access

The closest major airport is New Chitose Airport (CTS), Hokkaido's main international gateway, which sits within a manageable drive of Tomakomai. From New Chitose, expect roughly a 30-to-45-minute drive depending on traffic and the exact route to the course. From central Sapporo, plan on a longer drive of around an hour to an hour and a half, more in winter conditions or heavy traffic.

A rental car is the practical choice for this course — Hokkaido's train network thins out once you're away from the main JR lines, and a taxi from either New Chitose or Sapporo for the full distance will be expensive for a one-way trip. If you're not comfortable driving in Japan, check whether your hotel or the course itself offers a shuttle, or budget for a taxi arranged in advance through your accommodation.

## Dress Code & Tips

Standard Japanese golf club etiquette applies: collared shirts, tailored pants or golf shorts, and no denim on the course. Soft spikes are required at most Japanese clubs, including resort courses like this one, so confirm your shoes are compliant before you travel.

- Book earlier in the week for weekend tee times — resort courses in Hokkaido fill up fast during the short summer season.
- Build in extra drive-time buffer to and from New Chitose, since flight connections and course access both depend on the same access roads.
- Pack layers even in summer; Hokkaido mornings can run noticeably cooler than mainland Japan, and forest-lined holes hold shade longer.
