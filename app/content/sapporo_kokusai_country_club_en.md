## Course Overview

Sapporo Kokusai Country Club is laid out across rolling, tree-lined terrain typical of the hills ringing Kitahiroshima, with enough grade change to make club selection and shot shape matter more than raw length. The routing favors players who can shape a ball off the tee and control distance into greens that are kept firm and true for tournament play. Hokkaido's climate means tighter, slower-growing turf than you'll find on Honshu — fairways tend to run drier and truer in high summer, while spring and autumn rounds can play softer after rain. Wind is a factor on the more exposed stretches, and the surrounding hills can funnel gusts across certain holes, so bring a spread of ball flights rather than relying on one shot shape all day. Overall character leans toward a disciplined, championship-style test rather than a scenic resort stroll — it rewards accuracy and course management over sheer power.

## Access

The nearest major airport is New Chitose Airport, with central Sapporo itself serving as the more practical hub if you're staying in the city before or after your round. From downtown Sapporo, the drive to Kitahiroshima typically runs in the 30-to-45-minute range depending on traffic and route, while a transfer from New Chitose Airport runs longer, generally in the 45-to-70-minute range. A rental car gives you the most flexibility, especially if you're combining the round with other Hokkaido stops, but renting during winter months requires confidence driving on snow and ice. Taxis are a workable option for a single round trip from Sapporo but get expensive quickly for a return journey, so a private transfer arranged through your hotel or a golf travel agent is often the better value if you don't want to self-drive. If your itinerary includes multiple courses around Kitahiroshima, basing yourself in Sapporo and driving out for the day is usually simpler than relocating.

## Dress Code & Tips

Expect a traditional private-club dress standard: collared shirts, tailored or golf-specific trousers or shorts, and no denim on the course or usually in the clubhouse. Soft spikes are standard practice, and metal spikes are very likely prohibited — check before you pack. Pace of play tends to run deliberate rather than rushed, often with a halfway rest stop built into the round, so plan for a longer morning than a quick nine-and-done session.

- Confirm your visitor access route (introduction, agent, or platform booking) well before travel dates, since private clubs don't always take same-week requests
- Pack layers even in summer; wind off the surrounding hills can drop the temperature noticeably compared to downtown Sapporo
- Ask your booking contact about caddie requirements, since some private clubs in Japan still expect or require one rather than treating it as optional
