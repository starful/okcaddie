## Course Overview

Hokkaido Brooks Golf sits inland from Tomakomai in terrain shaped by forest cover, giving the layout a corridor feel where trees rather than bunkering do most of the defensive work. Expect the front nine and back nine to play with noticeable elevation change in places, which affects both shot selection and pace of play — plan for a longer round than a flat parkland course.

Turf conditions in Hokkaido generally favor cool-season grasses, which tend to hold up well through summer and give firmer, truer putting surfaces than the humid-season struggles common further south. Wind exposure varies hole to hole depending on tree cover; open sections can pick up more breeze off the surrounding Tomakomai plain, while wooded stretches stay calmer. Course character rewards accurate iron play into greens more than raw length, a common trait among Hokkaido's forest-style designs.

## Access

The nearest major air gateway is New Chitose Airport, which serves both Sapporo and the wider Tomakomai area — driving time from the airport to the course area is typically well under an hour depending on traffic and exact routing. Sapporo itself adds meaningfully more drive time if you're staying downtown rather than near Chitose or Tomakomai.

A rental car is the practical choice for golf trips in this part of Hokkaido; public transit options thin out once you're heading toward course access roads, and taxis over that distance get expensive if used for a full round-trip. If your itinerary includes other Hokkaido stops, renting from the airport and keeping the car for the golf leg of the trip is usually the smoothest plan. Winter driving conditions don't apply during the golf season, but early and late-season mornings can still be cool enough to affect tee time comfort.

## Dress Code & Tips

Standard Japan golf etiquette applies: collared shirts, tailored trousers or golf shorts, and soft spikes are expected at the clubhouse and on course. Metal spikes are typically not permitted, and overly casual streetwear (jeans, plain t-shirts) is usually turned away at check-in.

- Book tee times with buffer room — Hokkaido's compressed season means weekend slots fill faster than you'd expect for a course this far from the main Honshu hubs.
- Pack layers even in summer; mornings and shaded forest sections can run cooler than the daytime forecast suggests.
- Confirm cart vs. walking policy and caddie availability when booking, since forest terrain with elevation change plays differently depending on how you get around the course.
