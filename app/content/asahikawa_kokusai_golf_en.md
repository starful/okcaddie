## Who This Course Is For

- Golfers who want a genuine mountain-terrain test — elevation shifts and tree-lined corridors — without a premium green fee attached.
- Travelers building a Hokkaido itinerary around Asahikawa (zoo, ramen, Furano/Biei day trips) who want a relaxed round that doesn't eat the whole day.
- Value-conscious groups looking for a course that rewards course management over raw distance, at a price point friendlier than Sapporo-area resort layouts.
- Skip it if you're chasing a marquee designer name or a tournament-grade championship setup — this is a solid regional course, not a showcase venue.
- Skip it if you're traveling outside the Hokkaido golf season, since conditions and even operating dates can be limited by snow.

## Course Overview

Asahikawa Kokusai Golf sits in mountain-style terrain, meaning the routing leans on natural elevation change and tree lines rather than heavy bunkering or water hazards to create difficulty. Expect a mix of uphill and downhill approach shots that change club selection more than flat inland courses would, plus holes that frame views of the surrounding hills and forest — part of why "Scenic" is one of its defining tags. Turf conditions in Hokkaido generally lean toward cooler-season grasses that hold up well in summer but go dormant outside the short playing season, so fairway and green firmness can vary noticeably between early and late season visits. Wind is a secondary factor compared to elevation — it's present but rarely the dominant challenge here. Overall character is a fair, walkable-in-spirit test that rewards shot placement over power, well suited to mid-handicap golfers and those who enjoy reading a hillier layout.

## Green Fees & Booking

As a value-tagged public course, green fees here typically sit well below the premium resort tier — weekday rounds are generally the cheapest option, with weekend and holiday rates running higher, especially during Hokkaido's peak summer weeks. Packages usually bundle the green fee with a cart and sometimes a caddie or lunch option, depending on the day and how the course is running promotions that season. ## Access

The nearest airport is Asahikawa Airport (AKJ), which puts the course within a manageable drive of the city — realistically in the 20 to 40 minute range depending on exact routing and traffic, though you should confirm the specific transfer time when booking since it can shift with course access roads. From Asahikawa Station or central Asahikawa, expect a similar short drive. A rental car is the practical choice for this course: Hokkaido golf courses are frequently set outside dense public transit coverage, and a car gives you flexibility to combine the round with nearby sightseeing in Furano or Biei. Taxis are workable for a one-way trip but get expensive for a round-trip wait-and-return, especially with clubs, so most visiting golfers either self-drive or arrange transport through their hotel.

## Dress Code & Tips

Standard Japanese golf course etiquette applies: collared shirts, tailored golf trousers or shorts (no denim), and soft spikes only — metal spikes are not permitted at the vast majority of Japanese clubs, this one included. Bring a layer even in summer, since Hokkaido mountain courses can run noticeably cooler than lowland courses elsewhere in Japan, particularly in early morning tee times.

- Book early in peak summer months (July–August), when both Hokkaido tourism and the short golf season overlap and tee times fill fast.
- Confirm whether rental clubs and shoes are available if you're traveling light — availability and cost vary by course and season.
- Ask about caddie versus self-cart options when booking, since pace of play and service style differ and affect how long your round takes.
