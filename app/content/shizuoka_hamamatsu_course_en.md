Shizuoka Hamamatsu Course sits inland from the city, wrapped by mature forest that shapes nearly every hole. It reads as a traditional Japanese private club rather than a resort-style track, so plan your visit with that in mind.

## Who This Course Is For

- Golfers who want a genuinely local, members'-club atmosphere rather than a resort feel
- Players who enjoy tree-lined corridors that reward accuracy over raw distance
- Travelers already working with a Hamamatsu-area hotel concierge, golf agency, or local host who can arrange access
- Skip it if you need guaranteed same-week public booking with no introduction — this is not a walk-up course
- Skip it if you're chasing ocean views or open, links-style terrain — this is a wooded, inland layout

## Course Overview

Expect a layout carved through forest, with fairways framed tightly by trees rather than wide-open sightlines. That character rewards shot placement off the tee more than sheer power, and it punishes a wayward drive with a genuine recovery shot rather than a forgiving rough. Elevation change is moderate — enough to affect club selection on a handful of holes without turning the round into a hiking trip. Wind is generally muted compared with coastal Shizuoka courses, since the tree cover breaks up gusts, though open stretches can still catch a crosswind.

Turf conditions in this part of Shizuoka typically follow a warm-season grass on fairways with cool-season overseeding for winter color, which is standard for private clubs in the region — greens tend to run true but not unusually fast, and course staff can confirm current conditions on the day. Because it's a members' facility, maintenance standards are generally kept high and pace of play is managed carefully, so don't expect the loose, self-guided pace common at public resort courses.

## Green Fees & Booking

As a private club, Shizuoka Hamamatsu Course does not typically list open online rates the way public courses do — fees depend on your introduction route, day of the week, and season. As a general guide for private Shizuoka-area clubs:

- Weekday rounds via introduction: roughly ¥12,000–¥20,000 per person
- Weekend/holiday rounds: roughly ¥18,000–¥30,000 per person, when available to introduced guests
- Packages arranged through a hotel or agency often bundle green fee, cart, and caddie service; some include a locker and lunch

These figures move with season, holidays, and how the visit is arranged, so treat them as planning ranges, not quotes. The most reliable paths for visitors are a hotel concierge or golf travel agency with an existing relationship to the club, a personal introduction from a member, or in some cases a listing on a Japanese booking platform such as Rakuten GORA if the club opens specific dates to guests. Always verify a live quote before finalizing your itinerary, and confirm cancellation terms — private clubs can be stricter than public resorts.

## Access, Transport & Parking

The nearest major gateway is Hamamatsu, reachable by Shinkansen on the Tokaido line from Tokyo (roughly 90 minutes) or from Nagoya (roughly 40–50 minutes) to Hamamatsu Station. From there, the course is an inland drive of around 30–45 minutes depending on traffic and the exact route into the wooded club grounds.

A rental car is the most practical option if you're combining this round with other Shizuoka stops, since taxi fares for a one-way trip from Hamamatsu Station can add up and return pickups need to be arranged in advance in a low-traffic area. If you're not driving, ask your hotel or host to arrange a car service — many private clubs in this region are not well served by public transit, and same-day taxi availability near the club can be limited. On-site parking is available for members and introduced guests; confirm with your host whether advance registration of your vehicle is needed.

## Dress Code & Tips

Dress code follows standard Japanese private-club etiquette: collared shirts, tailored trousers or golf shorts, and soft spikes only — metal spikes are not accepted. Denim and sleeveless shirts are typically not allowed on the course or in the clubhouse.

- Arrive earlier than you think you need to — private clubs often expect guests in the clubhouse well before tee time for check-in and etiquette briefing
- Keep pace with your group and caddie; forested, tree-lined holes can slow play if you're searching for wayward shots, so play a provisional when in doubt
- Bring cash in yen for incidentals — caddie fees, locker tips, or small clubhouse purchases aren't always coverable by card at traditional clubs
