Mie Tsu Country Club sits in the wooded hills outside Tsu City, giving it a quieter, more insulated feel than seaside courses along the Mie coastline. It's positioned as a value option for golfers touring the Chubu/Kansai region who want a solid championship-caliber test without the premium pricing of marquee clubs.

## Who This Course Is For

- Golfers who like shot-shaping off the tee through forest corridors rather than wide-open target golf
- Value-focused travelers building a multi-course itinerary across Mie or Nagoya who want quality without top-tier green fees
- Mid-handicap and better players who want a legitimate championship test to sharpen course management
- Skip if you're a first-time golfer looking for a forgiving, wide-fairway warm-up round, or if you need guaranteed same-day walk-in access without any advance booking

## Course Overview

The layout leans on mature forest to frame most holes, so accuracy off the tee matters more than raw distance. Expect tighter driving lines than a resort-style course, with the trees also sheltering the course from strong crosswinds compared to coastal Mie tracks. Elevation change is moderate — enough to add some uphill and downhill approach shots without turning the round into a hike. Turf conditions in this part of Mie typically run firmer and faster in the dry autumn months, and softer with more mud after the summer rainy season, so bring an extra pair of shoes if you're playing in June or July. The "Championship" tag reflects a course designed to reward strategic play — layup decisions, correct sides of fairways, and disciplined approach clubs — rather than one built purely for scenery.

Visitors most commonly book through Rakuten GORA or similar Japanese golf reservation platforms, or through a hotel concierge if staying in Tsu or Nagoya. ## Access (アクセス)

The nearest major airport is Chubu Centrair International Airport (NGO), roughly 60-90 minutes by car depending on traffic and route. Tsu itself is served by JR and Kintetsu rail lines, with Tsu Station being the practical rail gateway; from there, a taxi or rental car is needed to reach the club, as it sits outside the immediate city center in a forested setting. If you're based in Nagoya, plan for roughly 70-90 minutes by car via the Higashi-Meihan or Ise-Wangan expressways. A rental car is the more practical option for golfers carrying clubs and playing multiple Mie courses on one trip; taxis work for a single round but add up quickly on a return trip, especially if you're staying overnight in the area.

## Dress Code & Practical Tips

Standard Japanese golf club etiquette applies: collared shirts, no denim, and tucked-in attire in the clubhouse and on course. Soft spikes are required at most Mie clubs, including this one — check in advance if you're bringing metal spikes, as they may not be permitted. Pace of play tends to run on the slower side during peak weekend windows given the forest layout narrows sightlines between groups.

Three practical tips:
1. Book your tee time and confirm cart/caddie inclusion before arrival — same-day changes are harder to arrange in Japanese golf culture.
2. Arrive at least 45-60 minutes early; check-in, changing, and warm-up follow a set rhythm at most Japanese clubs.
3. Pack layers in shoulder seasons — forest shade can run noticeably cooler than open fairways, especially on the front nine in early morning.
