Fuefuki sits in the Kofu basin, an area better known for hot springs and fruit orchards than headline golf, which is part of why rates here stay reasonable compared with courses closer to Mt. Fuji's base.

## Who This Course Is For

- Golfers who want a straightforward, well-maintained round without paying premium resort pricing.
- Travelers combining golf with an Isawa Onsen soak or a Yamanashi wine and fruit-orchard tour.
- Groups or couples looking for a relaxed pace rather than a tournament-tough test.
- Skip it if you're chasing a marquee designer name or a dramatic, close-up Fuji backdrop — the view here is more distant and weather-dependent.
- Skip it if you need trolley/cart flexibility identical to Western courses; confirm cart vs. caddie format before booking.

## Course Overview

Expect a layout that rewards accuracy over raw length, with the basin's relatively flat-to-rolling terrain keeping elevation changes moderate rather than dramatic. Turf conditions in this part of Yamanashi tend toward firm, well-drained fairways in the drier months and can soften during the June rainy season, so grip and roll will vary by trip timing. Wind is generally manageable but can pick up in exposed stretches, especially in early spring and late autumn. On clear days, look toward the horizon for glimpses of Mt. Fuji — the course's positioning inland means the mountain reads as a backdrop rather than a dominant, close presence, so treat it as a bonus rather than the main draw.

## Green Fees & Booking

Weekday rounds at courses in this Fuefuki/Kofu basin tier typically run in the roughly ¥7,000–¥12,000 range, with weekends and holidays climbing toward ¥13,000–¥20,000+; these are general estimates, not quotes. Packages usually bundle green fee, cart or caddie fee (confirm which applies), and sometimes a light meal, following common Japanese course conventions. Fees shift with season, day of week, and how far in advance you book, so always verify a live quote before finalizing plans. ## Access

The course sits inland in Fuefuki, with no major airport nearby — most international visitors fly into Haneda or Narita, transfer through central Tokyo, then continue overland. By train, take the JR Chuo Line limited express (Kaiji or Azusa) from Shinjuku toward Isawa-Onsen or Kofu, roughly 90 minutes to two hours depending on connections, then a taxi for the final stretch to the course. By car, the Chuo Expressway from central Tokyo toward the Isawa-Onsen or Katsunuma area typically takes about 1.5–2 hours outside heavy traffic. A rental car gives the most flexibility, especially if you're combining the round with onsen or winery stops, but a taxi from the nearest station is a reasonable option for a single golf day without a rental.

## Dress Code & Tips

Standard Japanese course etiquette applies: collared shirts, no denim, and tucked-in attire for men are the norm, with soft spikes required or strongly preferred at most clubhouses. Bring both, since rental options can be limited or run out on busy weekends.

- Book weekday slots if your schedule allows — pricing and availability are both friendlier than weekends.
- Confirm whether your round includes a caddie or is cart-only, since this affects pace and etiquette expectations.
- Build in buffer time for the drive or train transfer from Tokyo; rural station taxi availability can be thin outside peak hours.
