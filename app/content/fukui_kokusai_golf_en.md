Fukui Kokusai Golf sits in the hills outside Fukui City, giving it a quieter, tree-lined feel compared to coastal courses in the region. It's positioned as an approachable, fairly priced round rather than a marquee destination, which is exactly its appeal for travelers building a multi-course Hokuriku itinerary.

## Who This Course Is For

- Golfers who want a solid mountain-forest layout without paying premium resort rates
- Groups or societies looking for a relaxed pace and a course that rewards accurate iron play over raw length
- Travelers combining Fukui with Ishikawa or Toyama rounds who want an easy add-on stop
- Skip it if you're chasing a signature-designer showcase or expect ocean views — this is inland forest golf, not coastal drama
- Skip it if you need heavy English-language staff support without any advance arrangement, since smaller regional clubs vary in this regard

## Course Overview

The layout moves through wooded, hilly terrain typical of Fukui's inland courses, with elevation change shaping several holes rather than flat, open fairways throughout. Expect tree-lined corridors that demand shot placement off the tee, with forest framing most holes and occasional blind or semi-blind approaches where elevation shifts. Turf conditions on courses in this region typically transition between a firmer, faster feel in the drier late-spring and autumn months and slightly softer footing after summer rain, so fairway roll and green speed can vary by season and recent weather rather than staying constant. Wind is generally moderated by the surrounding trees compared to open coastal layouts, which makes club selection more predictable but also puts a premium on reading the terrain and slope around the greens. It's a course that favors course management and short-game touch over pure power, which is part of why it scores well with value-minded golfers rather than those chasing a bomber's paradise.

## Green Fees & Booking

As with most regional Japanese courses, Fukui Kokusai Golf's pricing follows a weekday/weekend split, and weekday rounds are typically the better value here. Rough estimate ranges to plan around:

- Weekday: roughly ¥6,000–¥9,000 per player
- Weekend/holiday: roughly ¥9,000–¥13,000 per player

These figures are planning estimates only — actual pricing shifts with season, time of day, and whether a cart or caddie is included, so always verify a live quote before finalizing plans. ## Access, Directions & Parking

The nearest major hub is Fukui Station on the JR lines and the Hokuriku Shinkansen, with the course a manageable drive into the surrounding hills from there. Komatsu Airport, the closest regional airport, is roughly 45–60 minutes away by car depending on traffic and exact routing, making it a reasonable day-trip or stopover course for travelers flying into the Hokuriku region. Fukui also connects to the Hokuriku Expressway, so golfers coming from Kanazawa or Toyama can expect drive times in the one-to-two-hour range depending on starting point.

A rental car is the practical choice — public transit doesn't reach most mountain-style courses in this area, and taxis for a round-trip from Fukui Station would be costly and need to be prearranged both ways. If you're not driving, ask your hotel concierge about shuttle or taxi arrangements well before your tee time, since same-day options may be limited.

## Dress Code & Tips

Standard Japanese course etiquette applies: collared shirts, tailored pants or golf shorts, and no denim or plain gym wear. Soft spikes are required at essentially all Japanese clubs, so confirm your footwear before you arrive if you're renting shoes locally.

Three practical tips:

1. Book your tee time and cart package together in advance — walk-up availability is inconsistent, especially on weekends and in peak spring/autumn weeks.
2. Pace can slow on hillier stretches, so build in extra time for the round versus a flat parkland course, particularly if walking any portion.
3. Bring layered clothing for shoulder-season rounds — mountain terrain holds cooler morning temperatures even when Fukui City feels mild.
