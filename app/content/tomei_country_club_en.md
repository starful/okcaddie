## Who This Course Is For

- Golfers who want a proper mountain test — Tomei rewards shot shape and club selection over raw length, so players comfortable reading slopes and adjusting trajectory will enjoy it most.
- Travelers basing in the Mt. Fuji / Shizuoka area who want a course with a real tournament pedigree rather than a standard resort layout.
- Groups arriving through a hotel concierge or golf travel agent, since private clubs in this region generally move faster through that channel than a cold call.

Skip it if you're a strict walk-up visitor without any introduction or booking partner — private clubs in Japan routinely turn away unaffiliated same-day requests, and if you need a flat, low-stress beginner round, a public course nearby will be a gentler starting point.

## Course Overview

Tomei sits in hilly terrain on the Fuji side of Shizuoka, and the layout leans into that setting rather than smoothing it out. Expect meaningful elevation change hole to hole, which means club selection on approach shots matters more than it would on a flat parkland course — a flier lie on a downslope or a held-up shot on an uphill green are both part of the day. Turf is typically kept firm and well-manicured for a club with competitive history, so run-out on tee shots can be longer than the yardage suggests when conditions are dry.

Wind is usually a secondary factor compared to elevation and slope reading, but mountain corridors can funnel gusts unpredictably on exposed holes, so it's worth watching flag movement before committing to a club. The overall character is tactical: narrower sightlines through trees on some holes, open valley views on others, and greens that tend to use the natural fall of the land rather than heavy artificial contouring. This is a course built to test shot-making and strategy rather than to overwhelm with sheer length.

## Access

Tomei Country Club is in Susono, on the Shizuoka side of Mt. Fuji, which puts it within a reasonable drive of both Tokyo and the Fuji Five Lakes area. From central Tokyo, figure roughly 1.5 to 2 hours by car depending on traffic through the expressway network; from Shin-Fuji or Mishima Shinkansen stations, the drive is considerably shorter, typically well under an hour.

A rental car is the practical choice here — the club's location outside a dense train network means taxis are workable for a one-way trip but awkward and expensive for a same-day round trip, especially with clubs. If you're not driving, arranging a taxi or private transfer through your hotel in advance is more reliable than trying to flag one down near the course.

## Dress Code & Tips

Collared shirts, tailored or golf-specific trousers (no jeans or athletic shorts), and soft spikes are standard expectations at a private club like this — pack accordingly, since rental options may be limited.

- Confirm your visitor status and tee time at least a few days ahead; private clubs in Japan often require this even when a booking partner has handled the arrangement.
- Build in extra drive time if you're coming from Tokyo on a weekend, since expressway traffic toward the Fuji area can back up around peak holiday periods.
- Walk the practice green before your round — on a course with this much natural slope, a few minutes reading break speed will save strokes later.
