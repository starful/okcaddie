## Course Overview

Aichi Nagoya Golf Club sits in the wooded terrain around Nagoya, with fairways cut through mature pine and mixed hardwood stands that give the course its forest-course character. The routing favors accuracy and shot shaping over sheer distance, with tree lines narrowing the effective width on several holes and putting a premium on tee-shot placement. Elevation change is moderate rather than dramatic — enough to add blind or semi-blind approaches in places, without turning the round into a hiking exercise. Because the trees enclose most corridors, wind is a lesser factor here than at coastal or open-country Japanese clubs; it becomes more relevant on the handful of higher or more exposed holes. Turf conditions follow the typical central Japan pattern — warm-season zoysia grass on fairways through summer, often overseeded or firming up in cooler months — so expect the ball to sit differently depending on the season of your visit. As a historic club, the layout reflects an older design sensibility: shorter, well-defended greens and doglegs shaped by the natural land rather than heavy earthmoving.

## Green Fees & Booking (グリーン料金 / 予約)

As a private club, Aichi Nagoya Golf Club does not typically operate an open public tee sheet, so pricing and availability depend on how you gain access. As a rough planning range, weekday rounds at comparable Aichi-area private clubs run in the ballpark of ¥15,000–¥25,000, with weekends and holidays closer to ¥25,000–¥40,000 — treat these as estimates only, since fees shift with season, member sponsorship, and any package terms attached to your introduction. A standard green fee package usually bundles the round with a caddie or cart fee and sometimes a clubhouse lunch, but inclusions vary by host and by day. Because this is an introduction-based club, the most reliable booking paths are through a hotel concierge, a golf tour operator with local relationships, or a member sponsor; general platforms like Rakuten GORA may occasionally list availability, but don't assume it will show up there. Always confirm the current quote and any dress or guest requirements directly with whoever arranges your visit before you travel.

## Access

The closest major airport is Chubu Centrair International Airport (NGO), with Nagoya itself serving as the main travel hub for the region. From central Nagoya Station, expect roughly 40–60 minutes by car depending on traffic and the exact route into the surrounding hills. A rental car is the more practical option for golfers, since clubs like this are rarely a short walk from a train station and taxi fares over that distance add up quickly, especially for a round trip. If you don't have a car, coordinate with your hotel or host about shared transport or a car service, since public transit alone is unlikely to get you to the clubhouse conveniently.

## Dress Code & Tips

Expect a traditional Japanese private-club dress code: collared shirts, tailored or golf-specific trousers or shorts, no denim, and soft spikes only on course. Clubhouse areas may expect slightly more formal attire for dining, so pack accordingly if you plan to stay after the round. Three practical tips: confirm your introduction and any host requirements well before the trip date, since private clubs often need advance notice rather than last-minute booking; ask in advance whether a caddie is required or optional, as policies differ from resort-style public courses; and build in buffer time for the drive from Nagoya, since traffic near the city can extend what looks like a short trip on the map.
