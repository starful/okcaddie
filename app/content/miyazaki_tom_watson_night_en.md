## Course Overview

Expect a resort-style layout built to work under lighting rigs, which generally means cleaner sightlines, well-defined fairway corridors, and greens sized for visibility rather than deception. Miyazaki's turf base leans toward Korai (zoysia) fairways, common across Kyushu resorts, which sit slightly tighter underfoot than bentgrass and reward a clean strike. Coastal Miyazaki air brings a steady, moderate breeze most evenings rather than gusty conditions, and the terrain is generally gentle — this is a course built for rhythm and pace, not steep elevation swings. Night lighting tends to flatten subtle green contours visually, so pace control on putts matters more than reading fine breaks.

## Access

The nearest airport is Miyazaki Airport (KMI), which has direct flights from Tokyo (Haneda), Osaka, and Fukuoka. From the airport, the resort area is roughly a 20–30 minute drive by taxi or rental car. If arriving by rail, Miyazaki Station connects to the JR Nichirin line from Fukuoka's Hakata Station, a multi-hour trip better suited to travelers already touring Kyushu than as a primary route.

A rental car gives the most flexibility for an evening tee time, since you're not depending on shuttle schedules after dark. Taxis are a reasonable backup if you're not driving — fares from the airport or central Miyazaki are predictable and short. Public transit options thin out in the evening, so don't plan on trains or buses for the return trip after a night round.

## Dress Code & Tips

Standard Japanese resort golf attire applies: collared shirts, no denim, tucked-in tops, and soft spikes only — most Japanese courses will turn away metal spikes at check-in. Bring a light layer even in warmer months, since evening temperatures drop noticeably once the sun is down.

- Arrive with extra time before your slot — night sessions run on tighter turnover than daytime tee times, and late arrivals can lose their spot
- Pack a small flashlight or use your phone's light for finding a ball that strays off the fairway edge lighting
- Keep pace of play tight; floodlit courses often have hard cutoff times for the lights, so slow groups get pressured to speed up or skip holes
