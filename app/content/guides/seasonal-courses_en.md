## Quick Facts

- **Who this is for:** golfers planning a Japan trip around weather, course conditions, and green fees rather than a fixed date.
- **Time needed:** 30-60 minutes of research per region before booking tee times.
- **Cost band:** green fees swing with season — expect low-season discounts of 20-40% off peak rates at the same course.
- **Best windows:** spring (April-May) and autumn (October-November) are the most reliable; summer and winter are workable with adjustments.

## Who This Guide Is For

This is for travelers choosing *when* and *where* to golf in Japan, not a single-course review. Useful if you're flexible on travel dates, comparing regions (Honshu vs. Hokkaido vs. Kyushu/Okinawa), or trying to dodge rainy season or snow closures.

Skip this if you already have fixed dates and just need a specific course's booking link, or if you're playing only one round at a resort that handles logistics for you — in that case, just contact the resort directly.

## How It Works / Steps

1. **Pick your travel window first, course second.** Japan's golf season runs roughly March through December in most regions, with Hokkaido's window shorter (May-October) and Okinawa/Kyushu playable nearly year-round.
2. **Check the rainy season (tsuyu) dates for your region.** Mid-June to mid-July is wet across most of Honshu; Hokkaido and Okinawa are exceptions with different patterns — verify live for the specific year.
3. **Cross-reference typhoon season.** Late August through September carries typhoon risk, especially in Okinawa, Kyushu, and coastal Honshu. Build a flex day into any itinerary in this window.
4. **Match region to season.** Hokkaido and Tohoku for peak summer heat relief; Kanto and Kansai for spring cherry-blossom-season golf; Kyushu and Okinawa for winter and early spring play.
5. **Book 2-4 weeks out for weekday slots, 6-8 weeks for weekend/holiday slots** at popular courses, longer around Golden Week (late April-early May) and autumn foliage peak (November).
6. **Confirm course-specific closures.** Some courses close for aeration/overseeding in early spring or late autumn — a 1-2 week window that varies by course and isn't always posted far in advance.
7. **Reconfirm within 72 hours of your round.** Weather, especially rain and typhoon tracking, can change fast enough to affect a round booked weeks earlier.

## Costs & Booking Reality

- **Peak season (spring/autumn weekends, Golden Week, holidays):** highest green fees, cart/caddie fees often mandatory, hardest to book.
- **Shoulder season (early summer before rainy season, late autumn after foliage):** moderate pricing, better availability.
- **Low season (deep winter in cold regions, typhoon-risk weeks):** steepest discounts, but weather risk is real — don't assume a cheap winter round in Tohoku will happen as planned.
- **What to confirm live before paying:** cancellation/rain-refund policy, whether caddie or cart fee is bundled or separate, whether the course requires a minimum group size, and current course conditions (some courses post real-time condition updates).
- **Common pitfall:** booking a course sight-unseen based on an old seasonal price list. Rates and surcharges (holiday premiums, weekend loading) change year to year — treat published numbers as a starting range, not a quote.
- **Common pitfall:** assuming all courses rent full gear sets. Club rental availability and quality varies more than cart/caddie availability — confirm if you're traveling without clubs.

## Regional Notes

- **Hokkaido:** short but reliable summer window; courses are built for heat relief, so this is the move if you're traveling in July-August and want to avoid Honshu's humidity.
- **Kanto/Kansai (Tokyo, Osaka area):** dense course supply means more flexibility, but also the most competition for tee times during cherry blossom season and autumn foliage weekends.
- **Kyushu/Okinawa:** the fallback for winter golf when the rest of Japan is cold or closed; typhoon risk is the main tradeoff in late summer.
- **Mountain/Tohoku courses:** often close entirely in winter — don't assume a course is playable just because the region is on your itinerary.

## Common Mistakes

- Booking a trip around cherry blossoms or foliage without checking that it also overlaps peak-price, hardest-to-book windows.
- Ignoring typhoon season risk for late-August/September trips to coastal or southern regions.
- Assuming a course's listed season/hours are current — aeration closures and schedule changes aren't always reflected in older listings.
- Not building a flex day into itineraries during rainy season or typhoon-risk windows.
- Comparing green fees across regions without adjusting for season — a "cheap" winter Kyushu round isn't directly comparable to a peak spring Kanto round.
- Skipping the 72-hour reconfirmation and getting caught by a late weather or course-condition change.

## Bottom Line

Choose your region based on season, not the other way around: Hokkaido for summer heat relief, Kyushu/Okinawa for winter, and Kanto/Kansai for spring or autumn if you can absorb the price and booking competition. Build in a flex day during rainy season (mid-June to mid-July) or typhoon season (late August-September), and reconfirm pricing and conditions live within a few days of your round.
