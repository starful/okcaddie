## Quick Facts

Hyogo Prefecture holds the largest number of golf courses of any prefecture in Japan, spread across the Kobe hills, Harima plain, Tajima's north coast, and Awaji Island. Best for golfers basing in Osaka or Kobe who want course variety without changing hotels. A single trip needs 3 to 5 days for 3 to 4 rounds plus transit. Cost band per round including cart and caddie fee runs roughly 8,000 to 20,000 yen on weekdays, higher on weekends — verify live at booking. Peak season is April to June and October to November; summer is playable but hot and humid, winter closes some northern courses.

## Who This Guide Is For

This is for golfers already staying in Kansai (Osaka, Kobe, Kyoto) who want to add one or more rounds without a long transfer, and for repeat visitors who've played the obvious Chiba or Shizuoka circuits and want a change of terrain. It is not for golfers who want a single flagship resort experience in one location — Hyogo's strength is density and variety, not one marquee course. If you have under 24 hours between flights, skip this and look at courses near Kansai International Airport directly instead of touring the wider prefecture.

## How It Works / Steps

1. Pick a base city first — Kobe, Himeji, or Toyooka — based on which sub-region's courses interest you, then work outward.
2. Decide rental car versus train-plus-shuttle. Most Hyogo courses run shuttle service from the nearest station, but schedules are limited to a few pickups per day.
3. Book through a Japanese golf reservation platform or call the course directly 2 to 4 weeks ahead for weekday slots; weekends often fill 4 to 6 weeks out in peak months.
4. Confirm whether the green fee includes caddie or cart, since Hyogo has both walking and riding courses depending on terrain.
5. Pack for elevation changes — hill courses near Kobe have real grade, while Harima-area courses are flatter parkland style.
6. Reconfirm your tee time by phone or app the day before; last-minute weather cancellations are common in the mountainous north.
7. Arrive 45 to 60 minutes early for the locker room and equipment check-in, standard across the prefecture.

## Costs & Booking Reality

Weekday green fees at mid-tier Hyogo courses typically land in the 8,000 to 14,000 yen range; premium clubs near Kobe or resort-style layouts on Awaji Island can run 15,000 to 25,000 yen, more on weekends and holidays. Caddie fees, when mandatory, add a separate charge per round — some courses are caddie-optional, others require one. Common pitfalls: booking a course expecting a shuttle from Osaka when the shuttle only runs from a closer regional station; assuming cart-only when the course is walking-and-caddie; and underestimating drive time between Kobe-area and Tajima-area courses, which can be 2+ hours despite being in the same prefecture. Always verify current pricing, caddie requirements, and shuttle stops directly with the course before finalizing a tee time.

## Regional Notes

Kobe and the Rokko mountain range host hillside courses with elevation changes and city or sea views, generally a short taxi or shuttle ride from Kobe Sannomiya. Himeji and the Harima plain offer flatter, more traditional parkland layouts, often easier logistically for a rental car loop. Tajima, in the north along the Sea of Japan coast, has fewer courses but a cooler climate in summer and closures possible in winter snow. Awaji Island, connected by bridge from Kobe, mixes resort-style courses with coastal views and works well as a half-day add-on if you're already crossing to Shikoku or Tokushima. This spread across four distinct terrains in one prefecture is the practical reason Hyogo shows up so often in course-count rankings — it's geographic variety, not one dominant cluster.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating "Hyogo" as one compact area and underestimating drive times between Kobe and Tajima.
- Booking a weekend slot at a popular Kobe-area course without checking availability weeks in advance.
- Assuming every course has caddie service or that every course is walking-only — confirm per course.
- Skipping the shuttle schedule check and arriving at a station with no pickup for hours.
- Ignoring seasonal closures in the northern Tajima courses during winter months.
- Not confirming whether quoted green fees include tax and caddie fee, which varies by club.

## Bottom Line

Hyogo's course count comes from stacking four different regional terrains — hills, plains, coast, and island — inside one prefecture, so the right move is to pick a sub-region first and book locally rather than trying to cover the whole prefecture in one trip. If your visit is short, choose Kobe-area courses for convenience or Harima for flatter, easier-to-book rounds, and save Tajima and Awaji for a longer or repeat trip.
