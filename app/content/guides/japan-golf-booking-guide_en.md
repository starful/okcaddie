## Quick Facts

This guide is for visiting golfers planning a round or multi-day golf trip in Japan without fluent Japanese. Expect to spend 30-60 minutes per course researching and booking, plus follow-up confirmation. Round costs typically range from budget public courses to premium member-guest clubs, with weekday rates lower than weekends. Best seasons are spring (April-May) and autumn (October-November) for weather and course conditions; summer is hot and humid, winter closes some northern courses.

## Who This Guide Is For

This is for golfers visiting Japan who want to book a course directly or through a package, and need to understand local booking norms, caddy/cart conventions, and common friction points for non-Japanese speakers.

Skip this guide if you're a member being hosted by a Japanese club contact — your host will handle booking and etiquette, and following their lead matters more than any general guide.

## How It Works / Steps

1. Shortlist 3-5 courses near your base (Tokyo, Osaka, Okinawa, Hokkaido) that accept visitor or public play.
2. Check each course's visitor policy — some are strictly members-only or require an introduction; public and resort courses are more open.
3. Book through one of: the course's own website (often Japanese-only), a hotel concierge, a golf-specific travel agent, or an aggregator booking platform.
4. Confirm whether caddies are mandatory (many traditional clubs require them) and whether cart-only self-play is offered.
5. Reserve tee times earlier for weekends and holiday periods — 2-4 weeks ahead is a safe buffer; weekdays can often be booked with less notice.
6. Get a written confirmation (email or screenshot) with date, time, course name in Japanese characters, and total cost breakdown.
7. Arrange transport — many courses are a taxi or shuttle ride from the nearest train station, not walkable.
8. Reconfirm the booking 1-2 days ahead, especially if it was made through a third party.

## Costs & Booking Reality

Course fees vary widely by region, prestige, and day of week; resort and member-guest courses sit well above municipal or public courses. Caddy fees, cart fees, and locker/shower charges are often listed separately from the green fee, so get an itemized total before you commit. Lunch is frequently bundled into the round at private clubs (there's typically a break between front and back nine) — confirm if this is included or extra.

Common pitfalls: booking platforms that only display in Japanese and auto-select add-ons you didn't intend; courses that require a local phone number for confirmation texts; and cancellation windows that are shorter than you'd expect, sometimes with fees charged close to the date. Always verify live pricing, cancellation policy, and payment method (cash-only is still common at smaller clubs) directly with the course or your booking agent rather than relying on a general estimate.

## Regional / Course Notes

Tokyo and Osaka-area courses tend to be a 60-90 minute drive or train-plus-taxi from the city center, so factor transit time into your day. Okinawa and Hokkaido resort courses often bundle golf with hotel stays and are more accustomed to foreign visitors, with English-language staff more likely. Rural and traditional clubs are more likely to require caddies, enforce dress codes (collared shirts, no denim), and have limited English signage — bring a printed or saved confirmation in Japanese to show staff if needed.

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming walking-only self-play is standard — many clubs require a caddy or a shared cart, which changes pacing and cost.
- Booking without confirming the total cost, then being surprised by caddy, cart, or facility fees added on-site.
- Ignoring dress code requirements and being turned away or asked to change in the pro shop.
- Underestimating travel time to the course and missing the tee time.
- Not reconfirming a third-party booking, leading to no record at the clubhouse on arrival.
- Expecting late cancellations to be free or easy — many clubs charge close to the date.

## Bottom Line

Book through a hotel concierge, golf travel agent, or an established aggregator rather than navigating Japanese-only club sites cold, and get an itemized cost and written confirmation before you travel. If a course won't confirm policy on caddies, carts, or cancellation in writing, treat that as a signal to pick a different course.
