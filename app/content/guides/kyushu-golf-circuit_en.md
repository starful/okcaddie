# The Kyushu Golf Circuit: A Fukuoka-to-Miyazaki Road Trip for Golfers

Kyushu, Japan's third-largest island, is built for a multi-city golf circuit. Its mild subtropical climate keeps courses playable well into winter, its volcanic terrain (Mount Aso, Sakurajima, Kirishima) gives courses dramatic backdrops most Honshu layouts can't match, and its four main golf hubs — Fukuoka, Kumamoto, Kagoshima, and Miyazaki — sit close enough together to string into a single road trip. This guide lays out a practical circuit and the courses worth building it around.

## Why Kyushu Works as a Circuit (Not Just a Single Stop)

*   **Mild climate almost year-round.** Kyushu's winters are noticeably milder than Honshu's, making it one of Japan's best off-season golf destinations, especially November through February.
*   **Volcanic scenery on nearly every stop.** Mount Aso in Kumamoto, Sakurajima in Kagoshima, and the Kirishima range near the Kagoshima/Miyazaki border all border golf courses directly.
*   **International flight access.** Fukuoka Airport has direct regional flights, making it a natural entry point before driving south through the rest of the circuit.
*   **Onsen at nearly every stop.** Beppu, Kagoshima's sand baths, and countless smaller hot spring towns line the route, so post-round recovery is never far away.

## Stop 1: Fukuoka & Northern Kyushu

Your gateway into the circuit, with strong courses close to the airport.

*   **[Fukuoka International](/course/fukuoka_international_en)** — A championship-caliber forest course in Munakata with real tournament pedigree; a strong opening round for the trip.
*   **[Shimonoseki Golf Club](/course/shimonoseki_golf_club_en)** — Technically just across the Kanmon Strait in Yamaguchi, but close enough to Fukuoka to serve as a seaside-links detour before heading south.

## Stop 2: Kumamoto (Mount Aso Country)

Kumamoto's courses trade ocean views for mountain and volcanic scenery.

*   **[Kumamoto Airport Country Club](/course/kumamoto_airport_country_club_en)** — A forest course in Kikuyo with a long tournament history and easy access if you're flying between circuit legs.
*   **[Kumamoto Chuo Golf](/course/kumamoto_chuo_golf_en)** — A challenging, value-priced forest layout also in Kikuyo, good for a second round in the area without a big price jump.
*   **[Kumamoto Castle Golf](/course/kumamoto_castle_golf_en)** — Mountain-style holes near Kumamoto City itself, useful if you want to pair a round with a visit to Kumamoto Castle.

## Stop 3: Kagoshima (Volcano & Onsen Country)

Kagoshima's courses play in the shadow of Sakurajima, one of the most active volcanoes in Japan, and the region's sand-bath onsen culture is unmatched anywhere else on the circuit.

*   **[Kagoshima Kokusai Golf](/course/kagoshima_kokusai_golf_en)** — Scenic mountain-style holes near Aira with strong value for money.
*   **[Kagoshima Airport 36 CC](/course/kagoshima_airport_36_cc_en)** — A large 36-hole facility in Kirishima, convenient for flight connections and flexible tee-time availability.
*   **[Kagoshima Takachiho](/course/kagoshima_takachiho_en)** — Also in Kirishima, with elevation changes and mountain scenery worth the short detour inland.

## Stop 4: Miyazaki (The Circuit's Marquee Finish)

Miyazaki is where the circuit peaks — home to Japan's most famous tournament venue and a genuinely subtropical, palm-lined golfing atmosphere.

*   **[Miyazaki Country Club](/course/miyazaki_country_club_en)** — An ocean-side, historic championship venue that has hosted JLPGA events; book well ahead for weekend slots.
*   **[Miyazaki Tom Watson Night](/course/miyazaki_tom_watson_night_en)** — A floodlit resort course that lets you fit in an extra round after dark, useful for maximizing a short circuit stop.
*   **[Miyazaki Lakeside Golf](/course/miyazaki_lakeside_golf_en)** — A relaxed, scenic lake-view round to close out the trip at accessible green fees.

## Suggested Driving Route

Fukuoka → Kumamoto (roughly 1.5 hours by expressway) → Kagoshima (roughly 1.5-2 hours) → Miyazaki (roughly 2 hours from Kagoshima, or fly Kagoshima-Miyazaki if pressed for time). A rental car is strongly recommended; Kyushu's expressway network connects all four hubs directly, and many of the courses listed above are not practical to reach by train.

## Planning Notes

*   **Best months:** October-May avoids the hottest, most humid stretch (July-August) and the worst of typhoon season (August-September).
*   **Book Miyazaki first.** It's the most in-demand stop on the circuit, especially around tournament dates and weekends — lock in Miyazaki tee times before finalizing the rest of the route.
*   **Leave a buffer day.** Kyushu's expressways are reliable, but volcanic activity around Sakurajima and Aso occasionally affects visibility or road access; build in slack rather than back-to-back same-day transfers.
*   **Rent clubs if flying between stops.** If you're combining a domestic flight leg (e.g., Kagoshima to Miyazaki) with the drive, shipping clubs ahead via takkyubin (delivery service) is often easier than checking them.

## Bottom Line

A Kyushu golf circuit rewards travelers willing to drive rather than stick to one city. Four hub cities, four distinct scenic identities (forest, volcanic, subtropical coastal, mountain), and a shared mild climate make this one of the most efficient multi-course trips in Japan — just make sure Miyazaki, the circuit's headline stop, is booked first.
