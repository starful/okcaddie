## Quick Facts

- **Who this is for:** golfers booking caddie-assigned rounds anywhere in Japan and unsure whether to carry extra cash.
- **Time needed:** 2 minutes to read, apply on every round.
- **Cost band:** caddie fee typically runs from roughly 3,000 to 6,000+ yen per round depending on region and course grade — always verify live at booking, since this varies by prefecture and season.
- **Season note:** caddie availability tightens on weekends and during peak spring (April–May) and autumn (October–November) foliage windows, so book caddie-assigned slots early in those windows.

## Who This Guide Is For

This is for visitors playing caddie-assigned courses (common at private and semi-private clubs, less common at public/resort courses that offer cart-only "self-play" rounds) who want a clear answer on tipping etiquette before they land. It's also useful for anyone booking through a Japanese golf reservation site where caddie fees appear as a separate line item.

Skip this if you've already booked a cart-only, no-caddie round — tipping questions don't apply there, and you can focus on cart pickup and pace-of-play rules instead.

## How It Works / Steps

1. **Check the booking confirmation** for a line labeled caddie fee (often shown separately from the green fee). If present, that fee is the full payment for caddie service.
2. **Do not budget a tip on top of the caddie fee** as a default assumption — standard practice in Japan bundles service into the fee, unlike US or UK caddie culture.
3. **Ask the pro shop or front desk on arrival** if you're at a club known for a more traditional or high-end service style; some private clubs have informal customs where a small envelope gift is appreciated, though this is not universal.
4. **If a gesture feels appropriate**, keep it modest and offer it discreetly at the end of the round rather than mid-round — never hand cash directly without an envelope at more formal clubs.
5. **Confirm cancellation and no-show caddie fee policies** at booking, since some clubs still charge the caddie fee even if you cancel late — this is a separate issue from tipping but often causes confusion for first-time visitors.
6. **Carry small cash regardless** for incidentals (locker, bag storage, vending) even if you decide not to tip, since not all clubhouses take cards for small purchases.

## Costs & Booking Reality

- Caddie fees are usually quoted per round, not per player, and may be split among a foursome sharing one caddie — confirm the split at booking.
- Some resort and public courses default to cart-only self-play with no caddie option at all; if you want the traditional caddie experience, filter for it explicitly when booking.
- Pricing sites and OTAs (online travel agents) sometimes bundle caddie fees into a "with caddie" package rate versus a "self-play" rate — read the fine print rather than assuming caddie service is included by default.
- Peak-season and weekend caddie fees can run higher than weekday rates; if traveling on a budget, ask whether a weekday or twilight slot reduces the caddie fee alongside the green fee.
- Common pitfall: assuming caddie fee = tip-inclusive everywhere. It's the norm, but a handful of high-end members' clubs have unwritten customs — when in doubt, ask the pro shop directly rather than guessing.

## Regional / Course Notes

- Traditional member clubs, particularly older courses with long-standing caddie staff, are more likely to have informal courtesy customs than newer public or resort courses.
- Hokkaido and rural regional courses often run cart-only or caddie-optional formats more frequently than courses near Tokyo or Osaka, where caddie-assigned play remains more standard at private clubs.
- Resort courses aimed at international visitors (Okinawa, parts of Kyushu) tend to default to self-play carts, which sidesteps the tipping question entirely — useful if you'd rather avoid the ambiguity.

## Common Mistakes

- Tipping by default out of habit from home-country golf culture, which can create an awkward moment since it's not expected.
- Assuming every caddie fee line item is identical across courses — always verify what it covers (forecaddie, walking caddie, cart-caddie hybrid) before the round.
- Forgetting that caddie fees can still apply on cancellation, separate from any tipping decision.
- Not asking the pro shop when a club's customs are unclear — staff will give you a direct answer if you ask before the round rather than guessing during it.
- Bringing only large bills for a courtesy gesture; if you do decide to offer something, small denominations in an envelope are more appropriate than a single large note.

## Bottom Line

Tipping caddies in Japan is not the default expectation — the caddie fee already covers the service, so budget for that fee rather than an additional tip. If you're playing a traditional private club and want to be safe, ask the pro shop about local customs before the round rather than assuming either way.
