## Quick Facts

This guide is for any visitor playing a private or semi-private Japanese golf club, where locker room and bath customs differ sharply from Western clubs. No extra cost is involved beyond your green fee — bath and locker use is typically included. Read time: 5 minutes. Relevant year-round, but bath etiquette matters most in summer (humidity, more bathing) and winter (hot bath is part of the round's appeal after cold mornings).

## Who This Guide Is For

This is for first-time visitors to Japan playing at member clubs, resort courses, or traditional clubhouses with a shared bath (ofuro) and locker area. If you're playing a Western-style public course with individual showers and no communal bath — common at some resort and municipal facilities — most of this still applies to the locker room, but the bath-specific rules won't come up. Skip this if you've already played multiple rounds in Japan and know the drill.

## How It Works — Steps

1. **Swap shoes at the entrance.** Most clubhouses require you to remove outdoor shoes at the entry and switch to house slippers before entering the locker room.
2. **Find your assigned locker.** Staff often assign a locker number tied to your reservation or give you a key with your tee time slip — confirm at check-in.
3. **Change into golf attire in the locker room, not the parking lot.** Clubs expect full change before you're seen on the course grounds.
4. **After the round, shower before entering the communal bath.** There will be a row of seated shower stations with a stool and handheld nozzle — wash and rinse fully seated before stepping into the shared tub.
5. **Enter the bath without a towel in the water.** Small hand towels are for washing or resting on your head, never dipped into the shared bath.
6. **Dry off before returning to the locker area.** Use the designated drying mat or towel area so the locker room floor stays dry.
7. **Return keys and slippers at checkout.** Some clubs charge a small fee for lost keys — ask if unsure.

## Costs & Booking Reality

Locker and bath use is almost always bundled into the green fee at Japanese clubs — there's rarely a separate charge. Rental towels, if not included, run a small fee (verify live at check-in, ranges vary by club). Some private clubs restrict bath access to members and their direct guests only, so if you're booking through a visitor package or agency, confirm bath access is included rather than assuming. Locker deposits (a coin or key deposit) are common at resort-style facilities — keep small change on hand. Tattoos can still be an issue at some communal baths tied to broader Japanese bathing culture; call ahead or ask your booking agent if this applies to you, since policies vary club by club and are not consistently posted online.

## Regional / Course Notes

Older, traditional clubs — common in Kansai and parts of Kyushu — tend to have smaller communal baths with stricter unspoken etiquette; staff may not speak English, so watching other golfers before acting is the safest approach. Newer resort courses in Hokkaido and Okinawa more often have private shower stalls alongside or instead of a communal bath, aimed at international visitors. Tokyo and Osaka-area clubs frequented by business groups often have more relaxed, faster-paced locker room turnover between rounds, since many members play early and head to work. In all cases, locker room quiet is the norm — loud conversation is uncommon compared to Western clubhouses.

## Common Mistakes

- Walking into the communal bath without showering first — this is the most common and most noticed error.
- Bringing golf shoes or outdoor shoes into the locker room instead of swapping at the entrance.
- Letting a towel touch the bath water, which is treated as unclean by other bathers.
- Assuming the bath is open to all guests when some clubs restrict it to members only.
- Leaving wet towels or gear on locker room benches instead of using provided bins or drying areas.
- Skipping the shower stool and standing to rinse, which can splash neighboring stations.

## Bottom Line

Shower fully before the communal bath, keep towels out of the water, and follow the shoe-swap sequence at the entrance — these three habits cover nearly every locker room mistake visitors make in Japan. When in doubt, watch what regular members do first and match their pace rather than guessing.
