## Quick Facts

Autumn foliage ("kouyou") golf in Japan peaks from mid-October through early December, depending on latitude and elevation. Hokkaido and Tohoku color first (mid-to-late October), Kanto and Chubu mountain courses mid-November, and Kansai/Kyushu foothill courses late November into early December. Plan for a half-day round plus travel; courses near colored foliage are often 60-90 minutes from the nearest major station or airport. Green fees during peak kouyou weekends run higher than off-season weekday rates — expect a premium, not a fixed number, and confirm live. This guide is for visitors combining a golf trip with leaf-viewing, not for players chasing championship-tier layouts only.

## Who This Guide Is For

This is for golfers planning a trip around late October through early December who want courses with strong tree cover, elevation change, or mountain backdrops rather than flat, open layouts. It suits travelers comfortable renting a car or using a hotel shuttle, since the most scenic courses are rarely walkable from a train station. It is not for golfers who need English-language booking support at every step — many clubs in these regions still take phone or Japanese-form reservations. Skip this guide if your trip window is fixed in summer or winter, or if you only want to play courses inside central Tokyo or Osaka.

## How It Works / Steps

1. Pick your travel window first, then match it to the region's typical peak — don't pick the course first and hope the color lines up.
2. Search for courses described locally as mountain ("yama") or highland ("kogen") layouts; these tend to have more deciduous tree cover than seaside or reclaimed-land courses.
3. Call or use a Japanese golf booking platform 2-3 months out for weekend peak-kouyou dates; weekday slots often open closer in.
4. Confirm cart vs. walking rules and caddie requirements — many mountain courses in Nagano, Nikko, and parts of Kyushu still require a caddie, which affects cost and pace.
5. Arrange transport (rental car or hotel-arranged shuttle) before booking the tee time, since public transit to these clubs is often limited or absent.
6. Pack layers: mountain courses in November can be 5-10°C cooler at the first tee than at your hotel, with cold mornings and mild afternoons.
7. Reconfirm the tee time and any dress-code or caddie details 2-3 days before play, since weather and frost delays are common in late autumn.

## Costs & Booking Reality

Peak-season weekend green fees at scenic mountain courses typically sit in a higher band than off-season weekday play — treat any number you see online as a starting estimate and verify current pricing directly with the club. Caddie fees, cart fees, and locker/shower charges are often listed separately and can add a meaningful amount to the total. Common pitfalls: assuming a club takes same-week bookings during peak foliage weekends (many are full), assuming English phone support exists (often it doesn't, so use a bilingual booking agent or hotel concierge), and underestimating drive time on mountain roads, which can double your expected transfer time versus a flat-map estimate. Always confirm cancellation policy and weather-delay rules before paying any deposit, since mountain courses are more exposed to frost-delay mornings in November.

## Regional / Course Notes

Hokkaido mountain courses color earliest and pair well with a late-September to mid-October trip, but some close for the season by late October — check closing dates before booking. Tohoku and Nikko-area courses (Tochigi, Gunma) are a common choice for Tokyo-based travelers wanting a day-trip or overnight kouyou round, with maple and ginkgo color typically strongest early-to-mid November. Nagano and Yamanashi highland courses offer elevation and Mt. Fuji or Japan Alps backdrops, with cooler temperatures extending the color window into late November. Kansai and Kyushu foothill courses run later, often late November to early December, and are a good fallback if your trip dates miss the Kanto peak. In all regions, courses built through valleys or along ridgelines generally show more color than coastal or links-style layouts.

## Common Mistakes

- Booking the trip around a fixed itinerary instead of the region's actual foliage timing, then arriving after peak color has dropped.
- Assuming a rental car isn't needed because "it's near a station" — many scenic clubs require a 20-40 minute drive from the nearest stop.
- Skipping the caddie/cart policy check, then being surprised by required fees or pace-of-play rules on arrival.
- Underpacking for cold morning starts, especially at elevation, where frost delays can push your tee time back.
- Not confirming whether the course is open that late in the season — some mountain clubs close for winter earlier than expected.
- Booking only one course for the trip; weather or frost delays can wipe out a single-round plan with no backup.

## Bottom Line

Match your travel dates to the region's real kouyou window first, then choose a mountain or highland course with strong tree cover in that region — don't reverse the order. Book 2-3 months ahead for peak weekends, arrange ground transport before confirming the tee time, and always verify current pricing, caddie policy, and season-closing dates directly with the club rather than relying on listed estimates.
