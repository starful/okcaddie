## Quick Facts

Shikoku is Japan's smallest main island, and its golf draws are the coastal and hillside courses facing the Seto Inland Sea to the north and the Pacific to the south. This guide is for golfers who already have a Japan trip anchored elsewhere (Osaka, Okayama, Hiroshima) and want to add 2-4 rounds of coastal golf without over-planning. Expect to need a rental car; public transit between courses is slow and often impossible for early tee times. Best seasons are spring (March-May) and autumn (October-November) for mild temperatures and lower wind risk; summer brings heat and typhoon exposure, winter is playable but windy on exposed coastal layouts.

## Who This Guide Is For

This is for travelers comfortable driving in Japan (or hiring a driver) who want scenery-forward golf as part of a broader Shikoku or Seto Inland Sea itinerary. It suits golfers who can be flexible on tee times, since many coastal clubs give priority to members and prefer advance calls over walk-ins. Skip this if you need everything bookable online in English, if you're not renting a car, or if you're only in Japan for a short Tokyo/Osaka trip with no time to detour to Shikoku — the logistics overhead isn't worth it for one round.

## How It Works / Steps

1. Pick a base city first — Takamatsu (Kagawa) and Matsuyama (Ehime) have the most course density and airport access.
2. Decide north coast (Seto Inland Sea, calmer, island views) versus south/Pacific coast (more exposed, more wind, fewer courses).
3. Rent a car at the airport; an international driving permit is required alongside your home license.
4. Shortlist 3-4 clubs within a 45-60 minute driving radius of your base to avoid daily long transfers.
5. Call or email each club (or use a Japan golf booking agent) 2-4 weeks ahead; many Shikoku clubs are not on major English booking platforms.
6. Reconfirm each round 2-3 days out — weather, wind, and member events can bump visitor slots.
7. Build in one buffer day for wind or rain, especially near typhoon season (August-September).

## Costs & Booking Reality

Green fees on Shikoku's coastal courses typically run in the mid-range for regional Japan golf — noticeably below Tokyo-area premium clubs, often bundled with a mandatory caddie or cart fee and a set lunch at the turn. Treat any specific yen figure you see online as a starting point only and verify live with the club, since weekday/weekend and member/visitor pricing differ. Common pitfalls: some clubs require a local dress code stricter than resort courses (collared shirts, no cargo shorts), some require reservations to be made in Japanese or through a local contact, and a few coastal layouts close or reduce visitor slots during member tournaments. Confirm cart-vs-caddie policy, dress code, and cancellation/weather-refund rules before you commit a tee time to your itinerary.

## Regional Notes

Kagawa-side courses along the Seto Inland Sea tend to sit on rolling coastal hills with sea views across to islands like Shodoshima, and are generally more sheltered from strong wind than the Pacific-facing side. Ehime and Kochi's Pacific-facing coastal courses offer more dramatic open-water holes but are more wind-affected, particularly in autumn typhoon season — check forecasts the week of play. Courses near ferry ports (useful if you're combining golf with an island-hopping Setouchi trip) can work well as a one-night detour: play a round, take an afternoon ferry, continue your island itinerary. Don't assume "coastal" means flat — several of these layouts have real elevation change between sea-view holes, so factor extra time per round.

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming a coastal course is bookable online in English — many require a phone call or agent.
- Under-budgeting drive time between courses on Shikoku's mountainous interior roads.
- Ignoring wind forecasts and packing only a light bag setup for what turns out to be an exposed links-style layout.
- Booking back-to-back rounds with no buffer day, then losing a round entirely to a rain or wind cancellation.
- Not confirming caddie/cart requirements in advance, leading to a surprise mandatory add-on fee at check-in.
- Treating Shikoku as a day trip from Osaka — travel time plus a round rarely fits comfortably in one day.

## Bottom Line

Shikoku's coastal golf works best as a planned 2-4 day add-on to a Seto Inland Sea or Shikoku road trip, not a spontaneous side quest. If you can rent a car, book 2-4 weeks ahead, and build in a weather buffer day, the north-coast Kagawa/Ehime courses are the more forgiving choice; if you want more dramatic open-Pacific holes and don't mind wind risk, look south toward Kochi.
