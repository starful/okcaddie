## Quick Facts

- **Who it's for:** first-time golfers in Japan, visitors unfamiliar with Japanese club customs, anyone booking through a hotel concierge or online portal for the first time.
- **Time needed:** 10-15 minutes to read this guide, plus 20-30 minutes earlier arrival at the course than you'd plan at home.
- **Cost band:** no extra cost if you already own standard golf attire; budget a rental or convenience-store top (a few thousand yen) if you arrive underdressed.
- **Season note:** dress code enforcement is strict year-round, but summer heat (July-August) and winter cold (December-February) change what "compliant" layering looks like — check course-specific notes before packing.

## Who This Guide Is For — and Who Should Skip It

This guide is for golfers who have never played in Japan and want to avoid being turned away at check-in or embarrassed on the first tee. It assumes you already know how to play golf — this is not a swing or rules tutorial.

Skip this if you've played multiple rounds in Japan already, or if your course has explicitly told you it's a casual public muni with no dress enforcement (these exist but are the exception, not the rule). Also skip if you're only driving a range or visiting a golf museum — the etiquette below applies to actual course play.

## How It Works — Steps to Follow

1. **Check the course's stated dress code before you book.** Most private and resort courses list requirements on their site or booking confirmation; call or email to confirm if unclear.
2. **Pack a collared shirt.** T-shirts, tank tops, and sleeveless tops are rejected at almost every course, including public ones.
3. **Bring soft-spike golf shoes.** Metal spikes are banned nearly everywhere; running shoes or sneakers are often refused at the bag drop.
4. **Wear golf-specific or tailored pants/shorts.** Denim and athletic shorts above mid-thigh are commonly disallowed; knee-length tailored shorts with socks are usually fine.
5. **Arrive 45-60 minutes before tee time.** Japanese courses expect check-in, locker use, and a visit to the starter's hut well ahead of the round — arriving "just in time" reads as rude, not efficient.
6. **Greet staff and other groups with a short bow or nod.** You don't need fluent Japanese; a simple "onegaishimasu" before the round and "arigatou gozaimasu" after goes a long way.
7. **Follow pace-of-play cues from the starter.** Many courses enforce a strict turn time at the halfway house; being told to speed up is normal, not personal.
8. **Repair divots and rake bunkers without being asked.** Staff notice this more than your score.

## Costs & Booking Reality

- Green fees vary widely by region and day of week; weekday rounds are typically cheaper than weekends, and resort courses charge a premium over municipal ones.
- Caddie fees, cart fees, and locker/shower charges are often separate line items — confirm what's included when booking, not after you arrive.
- Rental clubs and rental shoes are available at most resort courses but limited or unavailable at smaller rural clubs — don't assume you can rent last-minute.
- Many clubs require all-member or member-introduction booking; third-party booking sites and hotel concierges are the realistic path for visitors without a local member connection.
- Confirm live: cancellation policy, whether lunch at the halfway house is mandatory and billed separately, and whether the course requires a minimum group size (some require 2+ or 3+ players).

## Regional / Course Notes

- Resort courses near major visitor hubs (Okinawa, Hokkaido ski-season crossover regions, Kansai-area resorts) tend to be more visitor-friendly with English signage and relaxed rental policies.
- Older private clubs near Tokyo and traditional clubs in the Kansai region tend to enforce dress code and etiquette most strictly, including jacket requirements in the clubhouse dining room.
- Public (par-3 or short) courses are more forgiving on attire but still generally require a collared shirt and proper shoes.
- Halfway-house lunch stops are a near-universal custom on 18-hole rounds — budget time and a reasonable amount of money for this, it's rarely optional in practice even if not contractually required.

## Common Mistakes

- Wearing athletic sneakers instead of golf shoes and getting stopped at the bag drop.
- Showing up without a collared shirt, assuming a nice t-shirt will pass.
- Arriving right at tee time instead of 45+ minutes early.
- Skipping the bow/greeting and group etiquette, which locals notice even if they say nothing.
- Assuming clubs and shoes will be rentable on-site without checking ahead.
- Ignoring pace-of-play signals from the starter or marshal.

## Bottom Line

If you pack a collared shirt, soft-spike shoes, and tailored non-denim bottoms, and you arrive at least 45 minutes early, you'll clear check-in at nearly any course in Japan without issue. When in doubt about a specific club's rules, confirm live with the course or your booking platform before you travel rather than guessing from general advice.
